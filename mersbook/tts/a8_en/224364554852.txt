Title: Why Did BTS Refuse to Submit for the Grammy Awards?
A light topic for the weekend.

In the 1950s, people in the American music industry realized that while there were awards for movies (Oscars) and television (Emmys), there was no equivalent honor for music.

In 1957, music industry leaders came together in Los Angeles to establish the National Academy of Recording Arts and Sciences (NARAS).

NARAS officially named the award the "Grammy," a nickname derived from the gramophone.

This is why the winner's statuette is shaped like a gilded gramophone.

Today, the trophies are made from a zinc alloy plated with gold, rather than pure gold foil.

The Grammys work through submissions from record labels and Academy members, with NARAS members casting votes to select the nominees.

There is no public voting, and candidates are chosen by industry insiders regardless of chart performance.

The Grammys should be viewed by dividing them into General Field (main awards) and Genre Field (other awards).

The four main categories, known as the "Big Four," receive the most attention:

Record of the Year, Album of the Year, Song of the Year, and Best New Artist.

When you add Producer of the Year and Songwriter of the Year to the Big Four, they are collectively referred to as the main awards.

The main awards are voted on by all 15,000+ Academy members.

Thus, they carry the symbolic weight of being "recognized as the best by the entire music industry."

On the other hand, the Genre Field awards are decided by smaller panels of experts within each respective genre.

The biggest difference between the two is that the presentation of Genre Field awards is not broadcast on television.

Of the 95 categories, only 8 to 10 are awarded during the live evening broadcast.

The rest are handed out during the Premiere Ceremony a few hours before the main event, and the main broadcast begins in the evening after the stars arrive.

Achieving a "Grand Slam"—sweeping three or more of the Big Four at once—is considered the greatest feat at the Grammys, accomplished by artists like Bruno Mars (2018).

This shows that Bruno Mars, who sang "APT." alongside ROSÉ, is hardly a nobody.

While the main categories—especially the Big Four—are high-stakes battles competed before TV audiences across all genres, winning a Genre Field award serves more as a respectable line on a resume.

For the 2026 Grammys, five new Genre Field categories were introduced, one of which is Best Asian Pop Performance.

Because it is a Genre category, it is voted on by a small panel of experts rather than the entire voting membership, pits Asian music against other Asian music, and will be presented at the Premiere Ceremony rather than the live broadcast.

On July 29, 2026, all seven members of BTS posted the exact same message on social media simultaneously:

"We have decided not to submit for this year's Grammys. We hope our music is heard and loved for what it is, rather than being categorized by region or language."

BTS is currently at the peak of their career.

Their 5th full-length album, Arirang, released in March, topped the Billboard main albums chart for three consecutive weeks, while the track "SWIM" also reached the top of the singles chart.

Arirang qualifies for eligibility at the 69th Grammy Awards (releases between August 31, 2025, and August 28, 2026), but BTS voluntarily rejected the submission process itself.

They didn't avoid it out of fear of losing; they walked away at a moment when their chances of winning were higher than ever.

This is because the award they were likely to receive was not a main award, but the newly created Genre Field category: "Best Asian Pop Performance."

The Asian Pop category honors "works that significantly feature the language of one or more Asian countries, such as K-pop, J-pop, or C-pop."

Since J-pop and C-pop have a minimal presence in the US, the category was essentially created to target K-pop.

The Associated Press published an article pointing out that the newly established Asian Pop category acts as a type of "racialized barrier" for Asian musicians.

In other words, the move is being interpreted as another form of segregation designed to restrict non-Western artists from main categories like Album of the Year and Record of the Year.

Just one day after BTS announced their refusal to submit, Harvey Mason Jr., CEO of the Recording Academy, addressed the matter directly.

In a statement, he emphasized, "The Asian Pop category is not intended to separate or divide anyone."

He further explained, "Artists can certainly aim for both categories simultaneously," adding that submitting to the Asian Pop category does not disqualify an artist from main awards like Album of the Year or Song of the Year.

Technically, he isn't wrong according to the rules.

However, the core issue isn't about eligibility—it's about "categorization itself."

The moment a separate category exists, voters unconsciously funnel their votes there and refrain from voting for those artists in the main categories.

BTS took the Grammy stage for three consecutive years from 2020 to 2022, yet they never walked away with a trophy.

Let’s compare the Grammy winners from those years with BTS's chart performance at the time:


2021

BTS – "Dynamite": Billboard #1 for 3 weeks / Top 10 for 8 weeks

Lady Gaga & Ariana Grande – "Rain on Me": Billboard #1 for 1 week / Top 10 for 1 week.


2022

BTS – "Butter": Billboard #1 for 10 weeks / Top 10 for 10 weeks

Doja Cat & SZA – "Kiss Me More": Billboard Peak #3 (0 weeks at #1) / Top 10 for 19 weeks.


2023

BTS & Coldplay – "My Universe": Billboard #1 for 1 week / Top 10 for 1 week

Sam Smith & Kim Petras – "Unholy": Billboard #1 for 1 week / Top 10 for 10 weeks.


Despite being nominated five times, they never won once.

The Grammys essentially used BTS to boost TV ratings by having them perform, while denying them an award every single time.

It seems highly likely that BTS was outraged by the Grammys' subtle attempt to say, "Here's an Asian Pop trophy to keep you satisfied."

One-Line Comment
With BTS stepping out of the running, groups like BLACKPINK and Stray Kids are being mentioned as potential recipients of the new Asian Pop trophy. Whether people will cheer, "K-pop finally won a Grammy!" or feel disappointed, "It was only a side category, not a main award," at the February 2027 ceremony comes down to personal perspective. But seeing controversies like this submission refusal make major headlines only proves how much K-pop has grown.
