Alaska LNG Project
As always, let's go back to the beginning.
Indigenous peoples had lived in Alaska for a very long time.
In 1759, Russian fur traders entered Alaska.
At the time, Alaska was mainly used by Russian fur traders as a place to collect furs with the help of the indigenous population.
Spain also began sending expeditions to Alaska in 1775 and started claiming sovereignty over the territory, but there were no significant conflicts with Russia.
Neither country felt that Alaska was worth going to war over.
In 1819, Spain ceded its claims to territories north of the 42nd parallel to the United States and withdrew from the region.
At the time, Russia's attention was not focused on Alaska.
Russia was primarily interested in securing access to the Mediterranean.
After defeating the Ottoman Empire and gaining control of Crimea, Russia built up its navy and began expanding its territory toward the Mediterranean.
Russia began taking control, one by one, of small states such as the principalities of Wallachia and Moldavia, which were under Ottoman suzerainty.
Britain, which was trying to contain Russia's southward expansion, sided with the Ottoman government.
France, which was competing with Russia over influence in Palestine, also joined forces with Britain to prevent further Russian expansion to the south.
From 1853 to 1856, a war broke out between Russia and a coalition of the Ottoman Empire, Britain, and France.
Because much of the fighting took place around the Crimean Peninsula, it became known as the Crimean War.
In 1855, during the third year of the war, Russian Emperor Nicholas I suddenly died of pneumonia, weakening Russia's will to continue the war.
The war ended with the signing of the Treaty of Paris.
Over the three years of war, approximately 500,000 Russian soldiers, 170,000 Ottoman soldiers, 100,000 French soldiers, and 20,000 British soldiers died.
In the case of France, around 60,000 of the 100,000 deaths were caused not by combat but by infectious diseases. In Britain's case, approximately 17,000 of the 20,000 deaths were also caused by disease rather than combat.
In other words, the military doctors and nurses who participated in the war had an extremely difficult time.
Some nurses gained fame for their devoted efforts to care for the soldiers under such terrible conditions.
This was the background in which Florence Nightingale emerged.
After its defeat, Russia was forced at the Paris peace negotiations to restore its borders to their prewar status.
Russia also had to disband its Black Sea Fleet and dismantle fortifications along the Black Sea coast, suffering a number of penalties as the defeated power.
Under these circumstances, Alaska came to be seen as a remote and seemingly worthless territory that Russia could hardly afford to pay attention to.
After the Crimean War, Russia fell into financial difficulties and decided that it would be better to sell Alaska to the United States rather than risk losing it to Britain.
Russia approached the United States about selling the territory.
Eventually, Russia sold Alaska to the United States for $7.2 million.
At the time, American newspapers such as the New-York Tribune published scathing editorials criticizing the U.S. government's purchase of Alaska.
They argued that the government was wasting taxpayer money by buying a useless piece of land that had little more than fur-bearing animals, many of which had already been hunted almost to extinction.
For a long time, the U.S. purchase of Alaska was derisively called "Seward's Folly," after Secretary of State William H. Seward, who had negotiated the purchase.
Congressional approval of the funds needed to purchase Alaska was also delayed for more than a year because of opposition in the House of Representatives.
Alaska is roughly 17 times the size of South Korea and has a population of around 700,000, making it the least densely populated of the 50 U.S. states.
Because much of the state is still not connected by roads, air transportation accounts for about 82% of public transportation, and even pizza can be delivered by small aircraft.
For decades, Alaska was treated as a useless frozen wasteland.
But its value began to rise dramatically when large gold deposits were discovered in 1896.
Alaska's value increased even further after natural gas was discovered in Prudhoe Bay on the Arctic Ocean coast in 1968, followed by the discovery of some of the largest oil fields in North America.
The oil fields were developed, but because the oil reserves were located along the Arctic coast, the sea was frozen and oil tankers could not reach the area.
A pipeline had to be built from north to south, and commercial production could not begin until ten years after the discovery of the oil fields, in 1977.
In 1977, the 1,278-kilometer Trans-Alaska Pipeline was completed, connecting Prudhoe Bay to Valdez at the southern end of Alaska, and large-scale oil production began.
Prudhoe Bay contains far more natural gas than oil.
Since the existing oil pipeline cannot transport natural gas, a separate natural gas pipeline would have to be built.
The State of Alaska has been pursuing a natural gas pipeline project for many years, but the project did not fit with the Biden administration's environmental policies.
The situation changed when Trump became president.
One of the executive orders Trump signed on his first day in office was an "Executive Order on Unleashing Alaska's Extraordinary Resource Potential."
Trump approved the development of 28 million acres of oil and natural gas resources in Alaska and the Arctic Ocean that had been restricted under the Biden administration.
Local residents and politicians who wanted to see development in Alaska welcomed the move enthusiastically.
Crude oil has an export terminal at Valdez, while natural gas has export infrastructure at Nikiski on Cook Inlet.
If a natural gas pipeline is built from the Arctic coast to Nikiski, it could have a major impact on countries that rely heavily on natural gas imports.
These include Asian countries such as South Korea, Japan, Taiwan, and China.
Looking at a map, Alaska and Asia may appear far apart, but the actual distance is not as great as one might think.
The sea route from Nikiski, Alaska, to the Port of Busan is approximately 7,500 kilometers, whereas the distance from Qatar to Busan is around 11,700 kilometers.
If a natural gas pipeline is built from the Arctic Ocean to Nikiski, Asia, rather than Europe, would become the most logical export market because of Alaska's geographic location.
The problem is the $54.5 billion construction cost for the entire project, including the pipeline and LNG facilities.
It is estimated that the pipeline connecting Prudhoe Bay to Nikiski could eventually enable the export of 20 million tons of LNG per year.
South Korea imports approximately 47 million tons of LNG per year, so 20 million tons would be equivalent to about 43% of its annual imports.
Japan imports approximately 65.9 million tons of LNG per year, meaning that 20 million tons would represent about 30% of its annual imports—far from an insignificant amount.
This is only the amount of natural gas currently considered available from Prudhoe Bay. It is estimated that more than 4 trillion cubic meters of undeveloped natural gas remain in the Arctic.
So 20 million tons would only be the beginning, and additional supplies could potentially be provided if necessary.
On February 5, 2025, Alaska Republican Senator Dan Sullivan held a discussion with the Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS).
Senator Sullivan emphasized that it would be better for South Korea and Japan to import LNG from Alaska rather than from Qatar.
Sullivan said, "If Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan buy LNG from Alaska, they will be escorted by U.S. warships."
He also argued that U.S. LNG cooperation with South Korea and Japan would "be a response to China's Belt and Road Initiative," while the United States would reduce its trade deficit by $10 billion a year.
South Korea could participate in the construction of the pipeline and infrastructure for converting natural gas into LNG, and Korean shipbuilders could also be expected to receive orders for LNG carriers to transport the gas.
LNG carriers are built primarily by South Korea and China, but it seems unlikely that the United States would place LNG carrier orders with Chinese shipbuilders.
The Alaska LNG project is divided into two phases.
The first phase aims to supply natural gas to residents of southern Alaska by 2029 and begin exporting LNG in 2031.
From a political perspective, supplying natural gas to Alaskan residents by 2029 could have a much greater political impact than LNG exports beginning in 2031.
Before construction of the Alaska natural gas pipeline can begin, 80% of its annual production capacity of 20 million tons must be secured through long-term contracts.
As of March 2026, commitments totaling 13 million tons had been secured, leaving another 3 million tons needed to finalize the FID.
The 13 million tons include preliminary agreements for 2 million tons from France, 2 million tons from Japan, 1 million tons from South Korea's POSCO, 6 million tons from Taiwan, and 2 million tons from Thailand.
All of these agreements are conditional on the FID, and another 3 million tons of long-term purchase contracts are needed before the FID can be finalized.
FID, or Final Investment Decision, refers to the point at which the project formally commits to moving forward with the major expenditure and construction.
After obtaining permits and estimating construction costs, the project developers secure long-term sales contracts with buyers and negotiate loans and equity financing with banks and investors.
Once all of these pieces are in place, the project declares FID. From that point, final contracts are signed with construction companies, equipment orders are placed, and actual construction begins.
FID is similar to the point at which a real-estate development project moves from a preliminary financing stage to full-scale project financing.
Before FID, the project has generally spent only money on design and permitting, so it can still be relatively easy to cancel.
After FID, however, large amounts of capital actually begin to be deployed, making it much more difficult to walk away from the project.
Large projects often generate a lot of publicity but ultimately fail to reach FID and are abandoned, which is why the FID declaration is considered the most important milestone.
The Alaska natural gas project originally targeted an FID in December 2025, but it has not yet been finalized.
If South Korea provides investment capital and additional LNG purchase contracts, the remaining 3-million-ton gap and the construction financing could potentially be resolved, allowing the project to declare FID and move forward.
My own speculation is that South Korea may already have contracted for more than 3 million tons.
During Trump's visit to South Korea last year, he posted the following message on his social media:
"The Republic of Korea has agreed to pay the United States $350 billion in exchange for lowering the tariffs imposed by the United States. South Korea has also agreed to purchase large quantities of U.S. oil and gas, and wealthy Korean companies and businesspeople will invest more than $600 billion in the United States.
Our military alliance is stronger than ever, and based on this alliance, I have authorized South Korea to build nuclear-powered submarines instead of the old and less mobile diesel submarines it currently operates. It was a great trip and a great meeting with the great President of the Republic of Korea!"
Among the achievements of Trump's visit to South Korea highlighted by the White House was a long-term agreement under which Korea Gas Corporation would purchase 3.3 million tons of U.S. LNG per year.
This contract could potentially be for Alaska LNG.
The rapid pace at which the U.S.-South Korea investment announcements were made is also related to the U.S. midterm elections.
Republicans hope to retain a majority in the Senate even if they lose control of the House.
The Senate currently consists of 53 Republicans and 47 Democrats. If the two parties are tied, the vice president holds the tie-breaking vote.
For Democrats to gain a majority, they would need to gain four seats, not three.
In this midterm election, 35 of the Senate's 100 seats will be contested, including special elections in Florida and Ohio.
The remaining 65 seats are not up for election this time and consist of 31 Republican seats and 34 Democratic seats.
Of the 35 seats being contested, 22 are currently held by Republicans and 13 by Democrats.
Of the 22 Republican-held seats, 14 are considered effectively safe, similar to regions in South Korea where one party consistently receives overwhelming support. Nine Democratic-held seats are similarly considered relatively safe.
Republicans therefore have 45 relatively secure seats when their 31 non-contested seats are combined with the 14 safe seats being contested.
Democrats have 43 secure seats when their 34 non-contested seats are combined with the nine safe seats being contested.
After excluding the 23 safe seats from the 35 contested seats, 12 seats remain competitive.
Of those 12 seats, Democrats have a slight advantage in three—North Carolina, Georgia, and New Hampshire—while the remaining nine are extremely close contests.
The nine highly competitive states are Alaska, Iowa, Kansas, Maine, Michigan, Minnesota, Ohio (special election), Texas, and Nebraska.
If Democrats win all three states where they have a slight advantage, they would reach 46 seats and would then need to win five of the nine highly competitive seats to gain a majority.
Alaska is considered a highly competitive state, although polls often show Democrats with an advantage there.
If Republicans manage to win Alaska despite being at a disadvantage there, it would make it considerably more difficult for Democrats to reach a Senate majority.
This is why Alaska Senator Sullivan was present when Trump announced the Alaska LNG investment on September 30.
One-line comment
After experiencing a situation in which the Strait of Hormuz was blocked, the need for South Korea to diversify its LNG supply sources has increased.
At the September 30 announcement, Trump said that South Korea's investment would amount to $54 billion.
That figure is almost identical to the project's total estimated cost of $54.5 billion. If taken at face value, it would mean that the entire project would effectively be built with 100% Korean funding.
This is probably an example of Trump's tendency to exaggerate figures, but the final amount should be adjusted to an appropriate level during the final negotiations.
